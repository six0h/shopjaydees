# Messaging update and one decision for Jenn

Draft for Cody to present. Nothing in the "needs your call" section changes until Jenn approves it.

## Where we are

July's first two weeks of outreach reached 77 businesses with 134 personalized emails and produced 2 replies, both polite declines. That is a 1.5 percent reply rate against the 5 to 15 percent goal we set. The copy was doing what we told it to do, and that was the problem: we told Ellie "no pressure", gave her a soft ask ("worth a quick chat?"), and after the July 22 guardrails she had nothing concrete left to offer except the words "custom apparel". Polite, safe, and easy to ignore.

## What we changed (already built, inside your guardrails)

All of your July 22 rules still stand: no product names, no prices, no catalog, correct season only. Within them:

1. **Confident voice.** Ellie now writes like someone with a genuine reason to reach out this week, not someone apologizing for emailing.
2. **Real urgency only.** Every email anchors to the real fall calendar: orders that start now arrive before the season, orders that start later may not. Banned outright: invented discounts, made-up deadlines, "spots are filling up". If it is not the real calendar, she cannot say it.
3. **A real question at the end.** Every email ends with one specific question that can be answered in one line, for example "Want me to put a no-obligation fall quote together for you this week?". The soft closers are banned and machine-checked.
4. **The third email is now a break-up email.** Instead of a third "just checking in", Ellie says she is wrapping up her fall list and asks "Should I close the file, or do you want that quote first?". It is more honest, and in cold outreach it is reliably the email that gets answers.
5. **Two versions are now being tested.** Half the leads get an email that opens with the fall timing, half get one that opens with the personal observation and asks for the quote straight away. Each lead is tagged in ClickUp, so in a few weeks we will know which one earns more replies instead of guessing.

Nothing changes in your day: drafts still land in Ready for Review and nothing sends without your approval.

## Needs your call: one small loosening of the product rule

Right now Ellie may only ever say "custom apparel". That was the right call when she was inventing products, and the machine checks that stopped that are staying either way. But it also means a plumbing company and a soccer club get the same abstract noun, and abstract copy is easy to ignore.

**Proposal:** allow segment-level words only, so Ellie can say "crew gear" to a contractor, "team wear" or "warm-up gear" to a club, and "staff apparel" to an office or restaurant. Still banned, exactly as today: specific garments (hoodies, jerseys, toques), styles, fabrics, colours, prices, and anything Jaydees does not have ready to send.

Illustrative only (this does not exist until you approve it):

> With your crew back at full tilt this fall, matching crew gear is the fastest way to look like the bigger outfit on site. Want me to put a fall quote together this week?

If you would rather keep "custom apparel" only, that is completely fine; the new copy above already ships without it.

## One more ask: open tracking

Instantly's open tracking is currently off, so we cannot tell whether a quiet lead never opened the email (a deliverability question) or opened it and passed (a copy question). We recommend turning it on for the new campaigns so the A/B test reads cleanly. Small trade-off: tracking uses an invisible pixel, which very occasionally nudges spam filters; we think the data is worth it at our volumes.

## What happens next

1. New drafts start flowing through your normal Ready for Review queue with the new copy once the update is deployed.
2. In two to three weeks we compare reply rates between the two versions and keep the winner.
3. If you approve the segment-level wording, we ship it the same day behind the same machine checks.
