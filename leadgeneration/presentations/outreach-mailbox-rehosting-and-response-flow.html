Giving Your Outreach Addresses Their Own Home

We're moving your two outreach addresses off your main mailbox and into a separate, dedicated Google Workspace — so cold outreach can never touch your real business email. This guide walks you through the moves on your side, then shows how prospect replies reach you in ClickUp.

ellie@shopjaydees.ca ellie@shopjaydees.net

What's changing, and why

Right now, ellie@shopjaydees.ca and ellie@shopjaydees.net are aliases riding on your main mailbox, hello@shopjaydees.com. That works, but it ties your cold outreach to your main business inbox: they share one reputation, and replies and bounces land mixed in with your real email.

We're going to give those two addresses their own separate home — a brand-new Google Workspace, completely apart from your main one. After this:

  • Cold outreach sends from addresses that stand on their own — it can never affect the reputation of shopjaydees.com or your main inbox.
  • Each address warms up properly and independently, which means better delivery.
  • Your main inbox stays clean — no cold-outreach bounces or replies mixed in.

Your main email is never touched. Everything in this guide happens on shopjaydees.ca and shopjaydees.net only. hello@shopjaydees.com and everything on shopjaydees.com keeps working exactly as it does today — we never remove, move, or change it.

On cost: the new Workspace runs about $17 CAD/month for the two mailboxes. The $150 credit on your final invoice more than covers the first three months — so this change costs you nothing extra to get started.

There are six short steps. You handle the first five — they're done in your Google admin console and your DNS settings; Cody finishes the final hookup. Set aside about 30–40 minutes.

1 Free up the two domains You do this

A domain can only live in one Google Workspace at a time, so first we release shopjaydees.ca and shopjaydees.net from your current Workspace. (Again — this does not touch shopjaydees.com.)

  1. Sign in to the Google Admin console at admin.google.com with your administrator account.
  2. Remove the two aliases first. Go to Directory → Users, open the hello@shopjaydees.com user, and find the Alternate email addresses (aliases) section. Delete ellie@shopjaydees.ca and ellie@shopjaydees.net. Save.
  3. Remove the two domains. Go to Account → Domains → Manage domains. Find shopjaydees.ca, choose Remove domain, and confirm. Do the same for shopjaydees.net.

If Google won't let you remove a domain — it usually means an address is still using it. Double-check both aliases were deleted in the step above, then try again. If it still blocks you, send Cody a screenshot and stop here; he'll sort it before you continue.

2 Create the new Google Workspace You do this

Now we set up the separate home for the outreach addresses.

  1. Go to workspace.google.com and click Get started (or Start free trial).
  2. When asked for a business name, use something like ShopJaydees Outreach — this keeps it clearly separate from your main account.
  3. When it asks "Does your business have a domain?", choose Yes, I have one I can use, and enter shopjaydees.ca. (It's now free to use, because you released it in Step 1.)
  4. Pick the Business Starter plan — it's the lowest tier and all we need.
  5. Create the admin sign-in for this new Workspace. Use a different password from your main account, and keep these new login details somewhere safe — this is a brand-new, separate account.

Heads up: this is a second Google Workspace, separate from your main one. You'll have two admin logins now — your existing hello@ one, and this new outreach one. That separation is exactly the point.

3 Add & verify both domains You do this

Inside the new Workspace's setup wizard:

  1. Follow the prompt to verify that you own shopjaydees.ca. Google gives you a TXT record to add at your domain registrar (where you manage your DNS). Add it, then click Verify.
  2. Add shopjaydees.net as a second domain: in the new Admin console, go to Account → Domains → Manage domains → Add a domain, enter shopjaydees.net, and verify it the same way (another TXT record).
  3. The wizard will also show MX records (these route incoming mail). Add or confirm them as Google instructs for each domain.

Not sure about DNS records? This is the one fiddly part. If anything is unclear, send Cody a screenshot of where you manage your domain's DNS and he'll give you the exact records to paste — or hop on a quick call. Don't guess.

4 Create the two mailboxes You do this

These are real mailboxes now — each with its own inbox and password, not aliases.

  1. In the new Admin console, go to Directory → Users → Add new user.
  2. Create ellie@shopjaydees.ca (First name Ellie). Set a password and note it down.
  3. Add a second user, ellie@shopjaydees.net, the same way.
  4. Send Cody the two new addresses and their passwords (or share them however you prefer) so he can connect them.

Almost there — the mailboxes exist; one set of DNS records to add next, then you're done.

5 Set up email authentication You do this

This is the set of DNS records that proves your outreach mail is genuine and keeps it out of spam folders — SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. Since you manage the DNS, you add all three, on each of the two domains. Everything you need is below; Cody is happy to confirm any value if something looks unclear.

SPF — add this as a TXT record on the domain root (@):

  • v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

If an SPF record already exists on the domain, don't add a second one — a domain may only have one. Edit the existing record so it includes include:_spf.google.com.

DKIM — Google generates this one for you:

  1. In the new Admin console, go to Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email.
  2. Pick the domain, click Generate new record (use the 2048-bit key), and copy the TXT record it shows you.
  3. Add that TXT record at your DNS host (the host/name is usually google._domainkey), then return to the console and click Start authentication.
  4. Switch the domain selector to shopjaydees.net and repeat — DKIM is per-domain.

DMARC — add this as a TXT record at the host _dmarc on each domain:

  • v=DMARC1; p=none;

We start at p=none on purpose — it monitors without affecting delivery while the addresses warm up.

That's your part done. Once SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are live on both domains, let Cody know (along with the mailbox logins from Step 4) — the rest is his.

6 Cody finishes the hookup Cody does this

From here Cody takes over the final pieces:

  • Reconnects both addresses to Instantly — and because they're now real mailboxes, this uses the simple, stable Google sign-in (no more App Password workaround).
  • Runs warmup on both addresses for 2–3 weeks before any real campaign sends, building their sending reputation from scratch.
  • Confirms your SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are validating correctly before campaigns start.

He may ask you to approve the Instantly connection once inside the new Admin console — if so, he'll tell you exactly where to click.

Why not just CC your main inbox on everything? On purpose. Keeping cold outreach off hello@ is what protects your main email's reputation. ClickUp gives you one place to see and act on every reply — with the fit score and full history a CC could never show you.
